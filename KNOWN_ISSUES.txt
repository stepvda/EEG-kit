1. THE DRC GATE IS MET.  THE BOARD IS NOT YET RELEASED FOR FABRICATION.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ECO-EEG-016 section 3 sets the gate for releasing fabrication data as zero DRC violations,
every net one connected copper island, and both inner planes continuous under the analogue
zone.  ALL THREE ARE MET, on the census in section 0.

THE DATA IS RELEASED FOR REVIEW UNDER RFQ-EEG-002A.  FABRICATION RELEASE AWAITS THAT REVIEW.

That is a deliberate choice and not a formality.  Zero violations means the layout satisfies
the rules the programme wrote down.  It does not mean the layout is good.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE ROUTING HAS NOT BEEN REVIEWED BY A HUMAN LAYOUT ENGINEER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It was produced by the programme's own constraint-aware autorouter.  Every clearance has
been measured on the finished polygons rather than on a routing grid, and the DRC report
lists every relaxed connection by name, so a reviewer can go straight to the places where
the router had to squeeze.  That is not the same as somebody having looked at it.

169 connections were relaxed -- 36 on a narrower conductor and 133 at full width with a
reduced gap -- against the 19 the earlier DRC report listed (ECO-EEG-016 C-12; the count
moves with the route).  Every one is at or above the 0.20 mm minimum conductor and the
0.20 mm minimum gap (0.35 mm on electrode nets), so nothing is illegal and no rule is bent.
But a board that closes at minimum geometry in many places is not the same board as one that
closes at preferred geometry: thin conductors run hotter and etch less reliably, and minimum
gaps are where a fabricator's yield problems appear first.  This is the first thing
RFQ-EEG-002A should look at.

WHAT THE PACKAGE'S OWN CHECKS CANNOT SEE.  tools/drc.py checks copper and has no silkscreen
rule.  A pin-1 legend tick on the wrong end of a package -- which is what the SOIC-14
footprint carried until 2 September, and which would have destroyed three op-amps at first
power-up -- was invisible to it.  The specific defect is fixed in tools/fplib.py and
tools/simulate_production.py now proves every pin-1 mark is nearest pad 1, but the general
point stands: silkscreen correctness rests on one targeted check, not on a rule set.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE FIRMWARE IS BUILT AND HAS RUN IN AN EMULATOR.  NEVER ON HARDWARE.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built against a real ESP-IDF v5.2.5 on 2 September 2026 (target esp32s3, Phase 1
configuration).  The four images the flash step needs are in firmware/release/ with a
manifest.json carrying their SHA-256, which the production simulation verifies on every run.

IT HAS RUN, UNDER QEMU.  firmware/release/qemu_boot.log is one full boot cycle captured
from qemu-system-xtensa 9.0.0 (-M esp32s3, -m 4M).  What ran: the second-stage bootloader
read THIS partition table -- all nine entries, including the calib and prov partitions --
and loaded the app from the factory slot at 0x20000; app_main() reached its banner; the
microSD mount failed and the firmware continued with the warning it was written to give; the
ES8388 codec failed and degraded the same way; and the 6 MiB ring buffer would not allocate,
where FW-D13's NULL guard caught it and named the cause.  Three bring-up paths exercised,
three behaved as designed.

WHAT THIS IS NOT.  An emulator is not a unit.  QEMU's esp32s3 has no octal PSRAM, no
microSD, no ES8388 and no ADS1299, so every peripheral this firmware talks to is absent.
Nothing has been flashed to hardware, nothing has enumerated over real USB, and no register
value, daisy-chain order or SPI timing has met silicon.  The run proves the image boots and
that the code paths which do not need peripherals behave as written.  It proves nothing
about whether the instrument works.  (sdkconfig.qemu sets CONFIG_SPIRAM_IGNORE_NOTFOUND to
get past the missing PSRAM.  That overlay is for emulation only and is not a build
configuration for any unit.)

AND THE RUN FOUND A FAILURE MODE WORTH FIXING, WHICH IS STILL OPEN.  Two subsystems degrade
and one aborts: a missing card and a missing codec are warnings, but missing PSRAM calls
abort(), and the unit then reboots into the same abort, forever.  On the bench that is
correct.  In a participant's home it is the worst available behaviour -- a boot loop is
indistinguishable from a flat battery or a broken cable, it never enumerates, and the browser
tool that exists precisely to answer "what is wrong with this unit" can never reach it.  A
unit that cannot record should still be able to SAY it cannot record.  Raised against F-06
and TOOL-EEG-022; the fix is to hold the fault, enumerate anyway, and report it through
CMD_IDENTIFY.  It is not made.

STATIC IRAM IS FULL, WITH ONE BYTE FREE.  firmware/release/size.json reports 16,383 bytes
used of 16,384 on the linked image.  If that is the real limit, the next function marked
IRAM_ATTR fails the link with an error naming a section and not a cause -- and this design
has more interrupt work coming (E-13's tone scheduler, E-12's onset detector).  Turning off
the SPI-slave and gptimer ISRs, neither of which this firmware uses, did not move the figure
by a single byte, so it is not those.  Either the pool is genuinely full, or esp_idf_size is
reporting against a fixed 16 kB window that is not the limit on an ESP32-S3 with octal
SPIRAM and XIP.  Reading the linker map against hardware settles it; guessing from here does
not.  It is open item 1 of the production simulation and FW-EEG-001 does not mention it.

protocol layer passes a unit test suite; the whole exchange passes against a simulated

5. MET IN THE DESIGN.  NOT SIGNED OFF, NOT MEASURED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four things closed on 2 September 2026.  Each is closed in the design and none is approved
or measured, and those are different states that this package does not merge.

  S-02, SINGLE-FAULT PATIENT CURRENT, IS MET AT 36.8 uA against a 50 uA limit.
  ECO-EEG-024 is applied: R1-R16 are 68 kOhm, not 47 kOhm (tools/design.py:70-88).  It was
  53.2 uA, a 6.4 % overshoot on a SAFETY limit carried as not-met through three revisions.
  What it costs is written into the requirement it costs: E-10 states both branches and
  permits +/-1.0 dB for exactly this case, and the simulation confirms the 100 Hz response
  is inside it.  Same 0603 footprint, so the board did not move.  Normal-condition
  auxiliary current is 0.106 uA against a 10 uA limit.  NOT SIGNED OFF.  SR-01 is the
  electrical safety reviewer's finding, that reviewer owns its disposition, and the review
  has not started.  This is open item 6 of the simulation.

  THE BIAS-LEAD TOPOLOGY IS FIXED.  design.py's channel-11 exception is deleted: D11 and
  C11 now sit on BIASOUT, behind the series resistor, like the other fifteen channels
  (design.py:544-553).  Until that change the BAV99 clamp and the 10 nF filter on the
  driven bias lead to the Fpz forehead electrode sat on the PATIENT side, so a shorted D11
  half put a rail on the forehead electrode with no series resistor in the path at all --
  183.6 uA on this pack's bound B, unbounded on bound A -- and a shorted C11 bonded that
  electrode galvanically to AGND_REF.  RISK-EEG-011's SF-1a and SF-6a collapse into the
  ordinary SF-1 and SF-6.  Note that ECO-EEG-024 alone would NOT have fixed this: D11 had
  no resistor in its fault path, so a bigger resistor elsewhere changed nothing.  Getting
  it into the routed board cost a discarded full rebuild (63 of 145 nets) and three
  corrections to the surgical repair; tools/repair_nets.py re-runs the full DRC and
  refuses to write unless the board comes out at least as good as it went in.  It refused
  twice.

  E-27 IS MET IN THE FIRMWARE, AFTER A SECOND CORRECTION THE SAME DAY.  The bicolour phase
  driver is written.  It was first written to read both halves of the converter's lead-off
  word, LOFF_STATP and LOFF_STATN, with red as P AND N.  THAT COULD NEVER HAVE LIT RED.
  ads_init() enabled LOFF_SENSP only, so LOFF_STATN was zero on every channel for ever, the
  red term was always zero, and every site that had lost contact showed AMBER.  Enabling
  LOFF_SENSN was not the fix either: this board's montage is SINGLE-ENDED -- J2 carries IN1
  to IN8, one shared SRB1 and BIASOUT -- so there is no per-site negative electrode, and
  with SRB1 closed all eight N bits would have reported the one shared reference.

  FW-D17 replaced it with what the hardware does support: the positive-side comparator is
  read at TWO thresholds, COMP_TH swept between a sensitive and an insensitive setting.  Per
  site: trips neither = GREEN, trips the sensitive one only = AMBER, trips both = RED; green
  lit in phase A, red in phase B, amber in both.  The insensitive set is a subset of the
  sensitive one, so the three states are exhaustive and cannot overlap.  The alternation
  quantises to the FreeRTOS tick -- 2 ms half-phases on a 1 kHz tick, so about 250 Hz rather
  than exactly 240 -- which meets the "above 100 Hz" the requirement is written against, and
  is stated rather than rounded away.

  How it was missed and what now catches it: every check in the package asked whether the
  driver EXISTED, and it did.  simulate_production.py now resolves the colour expressions
  through their local aliases and fails if any colour depends on state ads_init() never
  enables; that check fails against the pre-FW-D17 source and passes against this one.  The
  first version of the check did NOT resolve aliases, found no g_loff_* names in `p & n`
  and passed the very defect it was written for -- which is the same lesson twice in one
  file.

  NEVER SEEN TO LIGHT.  No unit exists, so no light has been driven, and T11 -- which reads
  the R/G ratio with a colorimeter -- has not been run.  AND THE COLOUR BOUNDARIES ARE NOT
  SET: the two COMP_TH values are the ADS1299's documented endpoints, not measured trip
  points, so the impedance at which a site turns amber, and the one at which it turns red,
  are unknown until a unit is characterised.  T11's reference resistances are provisional
  until then.  Simulation open item 4.

  CMD_LIGHTS ANSWERS HONESTLY NOW (FW-D18).  The handler took c[1] as a plain enable and
  answered 0x00 OK to every value, so a host asking for mode 3 (force red) was told the
  request had succeeded while the firmware carried on showing automatic colour.  Modes 2, 3
  and 4 are implemented -- force green, force red, force amber, with an optional site mask
  in c[2] -- a mode the firmware does not have is refused with 0x0B UNIMPLEMENTED, and a
  payload with no mode byte is refused with 0x02 BAD_LENGTH.  Eight interop checks cover it;
  the harness compiles the real main.c, so they are checks and not restatements.

  E-11 IS MET, IN BOTH HALVES.  The Sallen-Key moves from X7R to C0G.  X7R is +/-15 % over
  temperature and f0 goes as 1/sqrt(C1.C2), so the corner ranged over 42.4 to 57.4 Hz
  against a 45 to 55 band: no build could hold it.  The obstacle was that 100 nF C0G is
  not a stocked 0603 part, so the network stopped asking for 100 nF -- capacitors down by
  ten, resistors up by ten: 10 nF and 22 nF C0G with 215 kOhm, f0 = 49.9 Hz, Q unchanged
  at 0.742, corner 47.5 to 52.5 Hz at C0G's +/-5 %.  The 10 nF is the same part the board
  already buys sixteen of as the electrode filter C1-C16, so it adds no purchasing line,
  and the footprints and nets are unchanged, so the board did not move.  NOT MEASURED:
  T12e reads it on a built unit.

TWO MECHANICAL FITS ARE IN THE SAME STATE -- measured on the solids, never printed.

  THE HM-04 / HM-05B SERVICE BAYONET ASSEMBLES AND TURNS.  Until 2 September the released
  meshes could not: the carrier's lug outer radius was 5.40 mm into a 5.30 mm slot,
  tangential clearance was zero in a material printed to +/-0.15 mm, and HM-04 had two
  straight axial slots and no circumferential run to turn into.  It is invisible from the
  key side, which is why nobody caught it -- HM-09's service key lugs are narrower and did
  fit, so the key worked on a joint the carrier could not use.  The re-cut is in
  tools/mech_gen.py: HM-05B's lug is 1.40 mm wide, 0.65 mm proud at r 4.55, outer radius
  5.20 mm, raised to z 1.20..3.30; HM-04 has 1.70 mm entry slots at outer radius 5.55, a
  100 degree circumferential run at z 1.10..3.80, and 1.10 mm of solid material below it
  as the retaining lip. tools/simulate_production.py intersects the two solids at four
  rotations and three axial positions on every run: no interference through the quarter
  turn, none through the carrier's 0.40 mm of spring travel, and measurable lip engagement
  when a seated carrier is pulled down -- the number that says an inverted helmet keeps
  its electrodes.  Reinstating the original lug centre makes that check fail, so it does
  not pass by accident.  NOT CLOSED BY THIS.  An intersection volume of zero says two
  solids do not overlap, not that PA12 at +/-0.15 mm turns by hand; the FIT-01 coupon
  remains the acceptance and none has been printed.  The cup pocket is still sized by what
  is left rather than by a cup, because HM-05A is a bought-in sintered cup "modified for
  the service bayonet" and the modification has never been drawn.  The cup contact crown
  and leaf are ASM-EEG-023's cannot-decide-here.

  THE FRAME NOW CARRIES ITS OWN HARNESS, IN THREE PARTS.  WH-EEG-008 section 7 gives every
  frame run two channels -- A on the outer wall for the electrode cable, B on the
  skull-facing wall for the light cable, 3.80 mm bore at 6.00 mm centres inside 1.20 mm
  walls, which needs a 12.20 mm section.  The carried-over HM-01 mesh has ONE bore per run
  in a 10.91 mm band: one channel where two are specified, and too narrow to hold the pair
  even if a second were cut.  That matters because the 6.00 mm of separation IS the
  ECO-EEG-014 rule keeping the light group away from the electrode group, and E-30 is
  written against it; a frame with one channel means both cables in one bore, the single
  arrangement the harness document forbids.  HM-01P-A, -B and -C now draw the halo,
  sagittal and coronal runs parametrically with the section the harness needs, and the
  simulation probes across the band for material on every run: 1.20 mm wall, 3.80 mm
  channel A, 2.20 mm centre web, 3.80 mm channel B, 1.20 mm wall, with channel centres 6.0
  mm apart.  THREE PARTS, NOT ONE, and honestly so: unioned into a single frame they came
  out as two disconnected bodies, because the three centrelines were each fitted
  independently to the v1 mesh and nothing ever required them to meet.  Joining them needs
  the head surface between them, which is exactly what the Stage 0 fit measurement holds
  -- and that measurement has not been taken, so the head-shape surfaces, the pad seats
  and the electrode site normals still come from the v1 study and will move when it is.
  This is a channel correction, not an ergonomic re-design.

AND ONE LIMIT EXISTS WITH NO MEASUREMENT BEHIND IT.  WH-EEG-008 justified leaving the
contact-light cable unscreened by asserting the lights are dark during recording blocks.
E-27 contradicts that: the lights are driven FROM the converter's own lead-off measurement,
so a lit light means a live measurement on the same electrode by definition.  The concurrent
case is electrode preparation -- the participant gelling a site and watching its light --
which is the state the lights exist for, and where the two conductors are closest.  The
design's controls are real and unchanged (two occipital entries, channels A and B at 6 mm
centres, an LED_GND guard conductor down the middle of the bundle, J30 in the digital zone,
source impedance below 1.1 kOhm).  What was missing was a limit and a measurement.
RFQ-EEG-001 E-30 now states the limit -- added noise below 0.5 uV RMS in 0.5-70 Hz, half what
E-03 allows the channel in the same band -- and TST-EEG-004 T9c measures it through the fitted
harness in an assembled helmet.  NEITHER HAS BEEN RUN.  Until T9c runs on a built unit, the
decision to leave WH-02 unscreened rests on an argument.
The same coupling problem exists at the site, and got the same answer: hm04() used to cut the
contact-light window as one box straight through the body, so the "inboard" and "outboard"
openings were two ends of a single cavity and any scheme putting the electrode conductor in
one end and the switched LED in the other had them sharing open volume about 3 mm apart at all
eight sites -- which is RISK-EEG-011 SF-9 exactly.  It is now two pockets with 1.60 mm of
printed PA12 between them.  Separated by material, not by distance, and also not yet measured.

9. ECO-EEG-023 IS SPECIFIED AND IS NOT IN THE DATA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The comparator U7 is powered from AVDD and AVSS in the released netlist (design.py:596-597),
and its 52 mV threshold is derived from AVDD.  ECO-EEG-023 would re-power it from
DVDD3V3/DGND and re-reference its inputs, which changes that threshold.

It is deliberately NOT cut in, and ECO-EEG-016 carries it as "OPEN, not implemented".  The
safety and layout reviewer must see it before it is applied; implementing it silently would
defeat the review.

10. THE .kicad_pcb ZONES SHIP UNFILLED, ON PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
kicad/EEG-CAR-01_RevB_routed.kicad_pcb carries the zone OUTLINES and the mounting-hole
keep-outs, but no filled_polygon cache.  A KiCad fill cache is one simple ring per island and
cannot express a hole; each inner-plane Gerber carries 698 clear-polarity void regions, so a
cache would have to be a solid rectangle -- which is what an earlier revision of the writer
shipped.  The DRC counts 4 pour islands in all, one continuous island per net per plane.

Press "Fill all zones" when you open the board.  The AUTHORITY for copper is kicad/gerber/,
which uses LPD/LPC polarity and carries every void.

11. WHAT A MANUFACTURER STILL CANNOT DO FROM THIS PACKAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On 2 September 2026 the package was audited stage by stage against one question: could a
competent contract manufacturer, given ONLY these files and no access to the authors, execute
the stage and hand on a correct result?  The answer was no, at every one of the six stages,
and the audit found thirty-two specific things that could not be done.

TWENTY-EIGHT OF THE THIRTY-TWO WERE CLOSED by writing the missing files -- ASM-EEG-023 Rev A
is the record.  What was written: the stencil aperture table, the IPC-D-356A netlist, the lot
coupon and lot documents, the ATECC608B configuration template and the provisioning
safeguards, a working provision.py --dry-run, the calibration schema and its worked example,
the per-unit record schema with an example record and a lot-summary template (records/), the
fixture PCB artwork and printed geometry and RP2040 firmware with passing host tests
(fixtures/), the FIX-02/D attenuator derived to E96 values, the label and packaging artwork
set (graphics/labels/), the HM-12 umbilical entry plate, the harness bus board, and the
mechanical release register.  The webtest deliverable was rebuilt from source, and the
TST-EEG-004 renditions now carry T30.

THE REMAINDER ARE NOT MISSING FILES.  They are missing DECISIONS, and section 12 is where
they live.  Two of them are not engineering questions at all and no amount of work in this
package closes them:

  * THE PROGRAMME'S OWN POSTAL ADDRESS AND TELEPHONE NUMBER appear in no file here.

12. TWENTY-TWO ASSUMPTIONS, DECIDED AND WRITTEN DOWN.  NONE APPROVED.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Twenty-two questions were decisions rather than files: a threshold voltage nobody had fixed,
a fastener nobody had chosen, a termination method nobody had drawn.  They are in
docs/ASM-EEG-023 Rev A, one entry each -- 8 derived, 11 judgement, 3 that cannot be decided
from what exists.  TEN BLOCK A BUILD.

Each was decided, then attacked by an independent reviewer, then re-decided against the
attack.  NOT ONE OF THE TWENTY-TWO SURVIVED THE ATTACK UNCHANGED, which is the most useful
thing to know about them.  Attacks were themselves checked before being conceded to, and
several were partly refuted on the files; those are recorded as kept.

ALL TWENTY-TWO REQUIRE A HUMAN SIGN-OFF and none is approved by that document.  A decision
with its reasoning attached is worth having because a reviewer reads a proposal faster than a
blank page, and because it makes an assumption visible instead of leaving it to be found by a
manufacturer at the point where it costs money.  It is not an approval, and this programme
does not confuse the two.

**Why this document exists**

RFQ-EEG-001 section 9.2 makes an electrical safety review the gate between two prototypes and
ten kits worn by human participants, and DSN-EEG-003 Rev C section 8 records that no safety
engineer has reviewed any of this design. Until now that gate had no input document and no
form: there was no hazard analysis, no patient auxiliary current calculation, no isolation
report, no checklist and nothing to sign. This pack is that input. It analyses the instrument
as an ISO 14971-style risk file, works the S-02 current budget through with the real parts of
EEG-CAR-01 Rev B, states what the isolation barrier is and where it lives, sets out battery
safety for a kit that travels by post, and ends with the checklist, acceptance criteria and
signature block the reviewer works to. It is written on the assumption the reviewer is a
stranger to the programme and hostile to hand-waving. **Nothing in this package has been
built, and no safety engineer has reviewed this design.** Nothing in it has been measured.
Every figure marked *calculated* is exactly that, and the review's first job is to decide
whether a calculation is enough evidence for the decision it supports.

**What changed in Rev B.** Two things about the carrier changed during layout and they change
parts of this pack. The board grew from 130 × 124 mm to **150.0 × 130.0 mm**, because thirty
connectors, 211 parts and 156 nets would not close at the smaller size. The board went from
two layers to **four** -- L1 signal, L2 reference plane, L3 reference plane, L4 signal --
because a two-layer carrier cannot be both a continuous reference under every analogue trace
and a second routing surface at the same time. Package v1 asserted that two layers would be
enough; doing the layout showed that it is not. Section 5 explains what four layers means for
the isolation argument, and it is more work to verify, not less: the keep-out under the
barrier must now be proved on four copper layers, two of which are invisible from the outside
of a finished board. Section 5.5 records what the design rule check now says about both halves
of that: the isolation rule area is clean on all four layers, and the report now records zero
violations of any rule, with all 145 nets connected. The fabrication data is **released for
review** under RFQ-EEG-002A. It is not released for fabrication: the routing was produced by
the programme's own tools and no human layout engineer has read it.

## 1. Intended use, users, use environment

### 1.1 Intended use

The EEG field kit is a sixteen-channel research instrument that records scalp EEG, facial and
laryngeal EMG, and three audio envelope channels, together with button responses and audio,
during a scripted listening and speaking protocol. It streams that data over an isolated USB
link to a browser-based session runner and writes an identical copy to a microSD card.

It exists to produce group-level data for one pre-registered study. It performs no analysis on
the device, applies no algorithm to a participant's data in real time, and returns no result of
any kind to the person wearing it.

### 1.2 This is not a medical device

The instrument has no diagnostic, therapeutic, monitoring, prognostic or screening purpose. It
is not placed on the market. It is loaned, not sold. It is not a medical device within
Regulation (EU) 2017/745, and the programme makes no claim that it is one. REG-EEG-012
carries the declaration, the reasoning and the controlled wording; M-03 puts the sentence
"research instrument -- not a medical device" on every enclosure label.

The design nonetheless follows the applicable principles of IEC 60601-1 for a type BF applied
part. Those two statements are not in tension and the reviewer should not read them as such:
the programme designs to a medical-electrical standard because the device touches a person for
two hours at a time, and claims no certification because it has no medical purpose and is not
being sold. Where this pack cites IEC 60601-1 it cites it as a design reference, not as a
conformity claim.

The word *patient* appears in this document only inside the standard's own term "patient
auxiliary current". Everywhere else the person wearing the instrument is a **participant**.

### 1.3 Users

| User | Who they are | What they do | Training |
|---|---|---|---|
| Participant | An adult who has consented to the study and reports voice-to-skull experiences. Not a clinician. Alone in their own home | Unpacks the kit, applies paste, puts the helmet on, follows the on-screen runner, presses buttons, ends the session, repacks and posts the kit back | A laminated card (IFU-EEG-014), an on-screen guide, and a support telephone number. No supervision, no site visit |
| Operator | Programme staff at Brussels | Refurbishes returning kits, replaces consumables and electrode cups with the HM-09 service key, runs the incoming test subset, despatches | Trained on SVC-EEG-013 |
| Manufacturer | The contract manufacturer | Builds, provisions, tests to TST-EEG-004 Rev C, packs | IPC-A-610 class 2, plus this pack for the risk-control components |
| Safety reviewer | An external engineer engaged by the programme | Section 7 of this document | See 7.1 |

### 1.4 Use environment

A domestic room. Uncontrolled temperature (assume 15 to 35 °C), uncontrolled humidity, mains
sockets of unknown quality, a mains-powered laptop of unknown make on the same desk, pets,
children, furniture, and no one else present. The kit travels between uses by courier in a
hard case, is handled by carriers who do not know what is inside, and is unpacked by someone
who has never seen it before.

Sessions are two to three per participant, each about two hours, of which up to two hours is
with the helmet on the head and headphones on the ears. A kit serves about twenty
participants a year in sequence, and more than that over the fleet's life; the arithmetic is
SVC-EEG-013 section 2, which costs fourteen days with a participant and about twenty
turnarounds per kit per year. The occurrence scores in section 3 are written against the
annual figure, and a multi-year fleet raises the exposed population above it.

### 1.5 Reasonably foreseeable misuse

Charging while wearing the helmet. Opening the battery hatch. Wearing it while cooking,
driving, or lying down. Wearing it overnight. Substituting other headphones. Letting a child
or another adult wear it. Immersing the helmet in water to clean it. Using the recording as
evidence of an external attack, and being distressed when told it is not.

## 2. Hazard identification

Hazards are grouped by energy or agent. Each group ends with the design property that is
supposed to hold it, so the reviewer can attack the property rather than the prose.

**Electrical.** The only mains-referenced conductor anywhere in the system is on the host side
of the ADuM4160 module at J10. The carrier has no host-side copper at all: J10's four pins are
VDD_ISO, USB_DN, USB_DP and DGND, all device-side (verified against design.py). The host
connection is a socket and not a captive cable -- the isolator module's own host receptacle,
USB-B on the only named candidate and brought to a panel USB-C by the WH-09 pigtail, which is
a live non-conformance against E-24 (section 5.4), presented through a gasketed aperture in
POD-P1 -- so exactly one externally accessible data connector exists on the pod. Everything
on the participant's side of the
barrier is a floating island powered by one 18650 cell. The credible electrical hazards are
therefore (a) failure of that barrier with a mains-referenced host attached; (b) a conductive
path that bypasses the barrier altogether -- specifically the ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1's own two
USB-C receptacles, which sit on GPIO19/20, the same pins the carrier taps for J10, and are on
the non-isolated side; (c) a fault putting an internal rail on an electrode; (d) the charge
input at J24, which is fed from a consumer mains adaptor whose isolation the programme does
not control.

**Thermal.** About 0.6 W dissipated inside a printed enclosure worn against the occiput, and
about 1.2 W while charging. Cell abuse is covered under battery.

**Mechanical.** A 1 m drop of a packed case. The chin strap. The 1.2 m headphone cable. The
printed bayonets and the frame edges. Pressure from eight spring-loaded electrode assemblies
over two hours. A crushed cell after a drop.

**Chemical.** Conductive paste and abrasive prep gel applied by an untrained person to their
own scalp, near their own eyes. 70 % IPA, which is an operator agent at refurbishment and is
deliberately not in the participant kit. Biocompatibility of the skin-contact materials listed
in REG-EEG-012 §6.2, none of which currently has a declaration on file. That list is eight
rows, this pack counts the same eight, and SR-05 is written against every row on it.

**Biological.** About twenty people a year, and more over the fleet's life, wear the same
frame, the same ear clips, the same boom and the same ear cushions in sequence, with paste
and abraded skin in the loop.

**Information.** A microSD card holding raw EEG, the participant's voice and room audio
travels back through the post. A unit can be confused with another unit, or its calibration
record attached to the wrong serial, which corrupts data rather than injuring anyone -- but the
study is the reason the device exists, so a data hazard is a real hazard.

**Psychological.** This cohort has been recruited because they report being acted on and
controlled by others at a distance. A strap under the chin, eight lights on the head, a
microphone pointing at the room and a device described as "recording your brain" are not
neutral objects to them. A red light, a failed session, a device that will not connect, or an
instrument that appears to switch itself on can be incorporated into a persecutory belief, at
home, at night, with no clinician within reach. This is not a soft category appended for
completeness; on this programme it is the hazard with the highest expected frequency.

## 3. Risk table

**Severity.** S1 negligible (no injury, momentary discomfort) · S2 minor (reversible, no
intervention) · S3 serious (reversible, needs intervention) · S4 critical (irreversible
impairment) · S5 catastrophic (death).

**Probability**, per participant-session. The whole programme is about 3,000
participant-sessions at 50 kits, so P1 means "not expected to happen once in the programme".
P1 improbable (< 1e-5) · P2 remote (1e-4 to 1e-5) · P3 occasional (1e-3 to 1e-4) · P4 probable
(1e-2 to 1e-3) · P5 frequent (> 1e-2).

**Risk index** R = S × P. **1--4** acceptable · **5--9** acceptable with the control in place
and the residual stated to the participant · **10--14** tolerable only on the reviewer's
written acceptance · **15--25** unacceptable, must be reduced before use on a person.

Probabilities are engineering estimates by the programme, not field data. There is no field
data. The reviewer may reject any of them.

## 4. Patient auxiliary current budget

This section is the calculation RFQ-EEG-001 Rev E S-02 points to, and it is the single home of
the series-resistor noise-and-flatness arithmetic that the rest of the package cites rather than
recomputes. **The resistor is 68 kΩ from 2026-09-02** (ECO-EEG-024); where a 47 kΩ figure appears
below it is labelled as the superseded one and kept only so the correction is legible. It is arithmetic on datasheet figures, not a measurement. Nothing in this package
has been measured.

### 4.1 Limits and applied parts

S-02: **≤ 10 µA DC** and **≤ 100 µA AC** in normal condition, including the lead-off
excitation; **≤ 50 µA DC** in single fault. S-02 states no AC single-fault limit; IEC 60601-1
allows 500 µA AC in single fault for a type BF applied part and this pack uses that figure,
flagged as an addition the reviewer must ratify.

There are **fourteen** patient connections: eight scalp electrodes (E_Fz, E_Cz, E_Pz, E_C3,
E_C4, E_T7, E_T8, E_F7), two ear references (REF_L, REF_R) and the bias lead (BIAS_EL) at
J14.1--J14.11, plus three EMG leads at the DIN 42802 sockets J15, J16, J17. That is
8 + 2 + 1 + 3 = 14, and fourteen is the number every document in the package uses; where
another document says thirteen it is wrong and is corrected. The two spare EOG channels
(EOGIN1/2 at J22) are protected identically but are not fitted to a panel socket in a standard
build; they are excluded from the sum and included in the fault analysis.

Every one of the fourteen passes through the same network: a **68 kΩ** 0.1 % 25 ppm thin-film
resistor (R1--R16, Vishay TNPW060368K0BEEA -- **corrected 2026-09-02**, ECO-EEG-024; it was 47 kΩ
TNPW060347K0BEEA until that date), a BAV99 dual clamp to AVDD/AVSS (D1--D16,
Nexperia BAV99,215 -- BAT54S is not approved in this position) and a 10 nF C0G capacitor to
AGND_REF (C1--C16, Murata GCM1885C1H103JA16D). The analogue rails are **AVDD = +2.5 V and
AVSS = −2.5 V** referred to AGND_REF, which is the analogue mid-rail and not ground.

**As of 2 September 2026 that is true of the topology as well as of the values.** Until that
date channel 11 was wired as an exception: `D11.3` and `C11.1` sat on `BIAS_EL`, the patient side
of R11, so the clamp and the filter of the bias channel were in front of its protection resistor
rather than behind it. The override in `tools/design.py` that did it is deleted and the row is
generated by the generic loop, so channel 11 now reads R11.1 = BIAS_EL (patient),
R11.2 = BIASOUT (module), D11.3 = BIASOUT, C11.1 = BIASOUT and C11.2 = AGND_REF, which is the
wiring of the other fifteen. R11 is 68 kΩ with the rest of them. **C11 stays fitted**, at the same 10 nF C0G Murata
GCM1885C1H103JA16D: RFQ-EEG-001 Rev E E-07 requires sixteen networks with C1--C16 all 10 nF C0G,
and depopulating one of them would breach a mandatory line of the top-level specification
silently. What the change does to the fault analysis is SF-1a and SF-6a of section 4.4; the
finding it closes in the design is **SR-12**, whose disposition is still the reviewer's to sign.

J15 to J17 are named in design.py as a Stäubli SLB1,5-F class part rather than a confirmed
PCB-mount catalogue item, and no touch-proof 1.5 mm socket with a PCB signal pin and two
retention posts has yet been first-articled. That is an open sourcing item on a Class A
patient-safety part and is carried in AVL-EEG-017; it does not change the arithmetic below,
because the socket is a connector and not an impedance.

cancellation is claimed.

**What the channel 11 change closes, and what it leaves.** It closes SF-1a and SF-6a, and it
closes them by moving two components rather than by argument: the fault paths those two rows
described do not exist on the board that `tools/design.py` now generates. It closes SR-12 **in the
design only**. The finding belongs to the safety reviewer of section 7, section 11 records its
disposition as theirs to write, and this pack may not record it as closed on the strength of the
programme having taken the option the analysis recommended. Two things travel with the change and
neither is a patient-current fault.

*The one thing the package cannot compute.* With C11 on BIASOUT the series resistor no longer
isolates the module's bias amplifier from 10 nF of C0G at its own pin, and no document here establishes that
the amplifier is stable driving it. The module is not bought, and the bias loop closes inside it:
ICD-EEG-006 §1.1 records at J2 that BIASOUT is an output of the module and that BIASIN is driven
by nothing on the carrier. No ADS1299 datasheet figure answers for a third-party module's output. It is therefore declared where this package declares everything it assumes about that
module: as a row in **ICD-EEG-006 §2.1's assumption table**, "Bias amplifier stable driving 10 nF
C0G at BIASOUT, with 68 kΩ in series to the electrode", with the
consequence if untrue that the bias loop peaks or oscillates and common-mode rejection degrades on
all sixteen channels. That is an incoming-qualification condition on a supplier not yet chosen and
a first-article scope check, and it is the **module-qualification owner's** to accept before any
PIEEG-8-class module is qualified, and to carry into the RFQ pack. The same §1.1 note is itself
owed a correction: it still records D11 and C11 as sitting on `BIAS_EL`, "the patient side of it,
where the other fifteen have their clamp and filter on the module side", and cites SF-1a, SF-6a
and SR-12 for the consequence. That sentence is superseded on 2026-09-02 and its repair belongs to
ICD-EEG-006's owner, not to this pack. The safety exposure if the assumption is wrong is bounded and
functional, not a fault current: an oscillating bias amplifier is rail-limited and reaches the

## 5. Creepage, clearance, and why the carrier has none to check

### 5.1 Where the barrier is

The barrier is entirely inside the ADuM4160 module at J10. The host receptacle is on the
module -- USB-B on the only named candidate, brought to a panel USB-C by the WH-09 pigtail,
which is a live non-conformance against E-24 and is stated as such in section 5.4. The four
carrier pins are `VDD_ISO`, `USB_DN`, `USB_DP`, `DGND` -- every one of them on the device
side. There is no host-side net anywhere in design.py's netlist, on any of the four
layers, at any coordinate. **The carrier has no isolation barrier, and therefore has no
creepage or clearance across one to assess.** That is the whole isolation strategy in one
sentence, and it is the reason the carrier needs no milled creepage slot: not because of how
many layers it has, but because no conductor on it is ever mains-referenced.

The layer count changed during layout and the reviewer should know what it does and does not
change here. EEG-CAR-01 Rev B is a **four-layer** board -- L1 signal, L2 reference plane, L3
reference plane, L4 signal, with AGND_REF left of x = 62 mm and DGND right of it on both inner
layers, stitched together only at the R90 star point. Package v1 argued that two layers would
be enough; doing the layout showed that on two layers the bottom side would have to be both
the reference plane and the second routing surface, and it cannot be both. For the isolation
case the four-layer change is neutral in principle and adds work in practice: there are now
**four** copper layers on which the keep-out under the barrier must be proved, and two of them
cannot be inspected on a finished board. Two properties make that verifiable rather than
hopeful. Vias are **through vias only** -- no blind, buried or back-drilled structures -- so
every hole in the barrier region appears in the drill file and on both outer plots. And the
keep-out is encoded as a rule area on all four copper layers, so the DRC proves it rather than
a person measuring a plot. In the Rev B routing it does: `kicad/EEG-CAR-01_RevB_DRC_report.txt`
lists no isolation keep-out violation on any of the four layers. Section 5.5 gives that result
with its figures, and states what the same report does and does not settle.

The consequence, which the reviewer should hold on to: the entire safety case for S-03 rests on
a purchased breakout board whose layout the programme does not control.

### 5.2 What the barrier must achieve

Working voltage on the host side is mains, taken as 250 V RMS, pollution degree 2, material
group IIIb unless the module vendor declares better.

### 5.4 The slant path, and what is still not fixed

### 6.2 Charge control

Charging is by a **separate, charge-only USB-C receptacle** on the panel, wired through a JST
PH pigtail to J24. No data conductor enters it. The path is F1 (Bourns MF-MSMF110-2 PTC,
1.1 A hold / 2.2 A trip) then D24 (Nexperia PESD5V0S2BT TVS) to VBUS_CHG at J12.3, with the
R84/R85 divider producing VBUS_DET at GPIO46. R84 is 100 kΩ and **R85 is 150 kΩ**, giving
5 × 150/250 = **3.00 V** at VBUS = 5 V, comfortably above the 2.48 V input-high threshold of a
3.3 V ESP32-S3 input; the 56 kΩ of the first cut gave 1.79 V and would not have asserted the
first S-01 interlock reliably, which is ECO-EEG-022. This receptacle is galvanically separate
from the isolated data USB-C on the ADuM4160 module: ECO-EEG-003 exists precisely because Rev A
tried to make one connector do both, which cannot be reconciled with S-03.

The interlock (S-01) has two mechanisms:

1. firmware refuses `CMD_START_SESSION` while VBUS_DET is high, with a defined ACK code;
2. CHG_CE (GPIO47, at J12.4) holds the charger disabled for the whole of a session.

Both are verified per unit at T4 and T21. **Both are firmware.** There is no hardware gate
that would keep the charger off if the firmware misbehaved, and section 3 records that as H-08
with a decision owed at SR-02. The reviewer must rule on whether a firmware-only interlock is
acceptable for a device used unsupervised, or whether an independent hardware inhibit -- a
two-input gate between VBUS_DET, a hardware session-active latch and CHG_CE -- is required
before Phase 2. It is two parts on a board that has not been fabricated.

1. **At the door.** Goods-in opens every returning case in a designated area away from
   combustibles, with a lithium-fire bucket to hand. Any case that is hot, deformed, smells,
   or was reported as dropped is not opened indoors.
2. **Quarantine on sight.** A swollen, dented, discoloured or leaking cell, or a cell whose
   wrapper is torn, is a **quarantine, no-rework** disposition under QP-EEG-010. The cell is
   not charged, not tested and not re-used. It goes to a metal container with a lid, in sand,
   outside, pending disposal through the programme's WEEE and battery route.
3. **The kit follows the cell.** A kit that carried a damaged cell does not re-enter
   circulation on a visual check. It gets the full incoming subset (T3, T7, T8, T10, T13,
   T17), plus T20 insulation resistance, plus an inspection of J13, the HM-10 carrier, the
   carrier board around J13 and the HM-08 hatch, before it is released.
4. **It becomes evidence.** Every damaged-cell return is a nonconformance that feeds this risk
   file's post-production loop (section 7.6). Two in a fleet year is a signal about the case,
   the foam or the retention, not about one participant.
5. **The participant is told they did nothing wrong**, in those words, before anything else is
   said to them. On this cohort, a message that reads as blame for damaging a device that
   "records the brain" is a harm in itself.
